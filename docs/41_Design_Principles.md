# PawGuard Design Principles

> Derived from Visual Research Lab analysis (Cards 001–015)
> These principles are evidence-based, not preference-based.

---

## Composition Principles

### Principle 001: Single Focal Point First
**The Hero should establish one dominant focal point before introducing any supporting information.**

The eye must land somewhere specific within 200ms. If the viewer has to decide where to look, the composition has failed. The dominant element should be 3-5x larger than the next element, creating unambiguous visual hierarchy.

*Source: Card 001 (Apple Vision Pro), Card 005 (Linear.app)*

---

### Principle 002: Three-Zone Hierarchy
**Every Hero has three zones: Emotional (primary), Informational (secondary), Action (tertiary).**

Each zone has distinct visual weight. The emotional zone (photograph) should occupy 40-50% of visual attention. The informational zone (headline + text) should occupy 30-35%. The action zone (CTAs) should occupy 15-20%. Everything else shares the remaining 5-10%.

*Source: Card 003 (Stripe)*

---

### Principle 003: Negative Space as Confidence
**What you leave out is as important as what you include.**

Empty space says "we're confident enough to let this breathe." Cluttered compositions communicate anxiety. The Hero should feel intentional, not accidental. Every element should have room to exist without competing.

*Source: Card 004 (A24 Poster), Card 011 (Kinfolk Magazine)*

---

### Principle 004: Asymmetric Balance
**Symmetric compositions feel static. Asymmetric compositions feel dynamic.**

The Hero should use asymmetric layouts — content weighted to one side, visual weight balanced on the other. This creates visual tension that holds attention without creating imbalance.

*Source: Card 001, Card 003*

---

## Photography Principles

### Principle 005: Documentary Authenticity
**Real moments create trust. Staged moments erode it.**

Every photograph should feel like a witnessed moment, not a performed one. Available light, natural environments, candid interactions. The viewer should feel like they're seeing something genuine.

*Source: Card 006 (ASPCA), Card 007 (National Geographic)*

---

### Principle 006: Quiet Connection Over Drama
**Restraint is more trustworthy than sentimentality.**

The most powerful documentary images show quiet competence and genuine connection — not dramatic rescues or forced emotions. A volunteer gently examining a dog communicates more trust than a heroic "saving" pose.

*Source: Card 007 (Ami Vitale), Card 008 (Sophie Gamand)*

---

### Principle 007: Individualization Creates Empathy
**When a subject has a name and a face, the viewer connects personally.**

Generic images create generic emotions. Every animal in the Hero should feel like THIS animal, not "a dog." This means specific, detailed imagery rather than generic pet photography.

*Source: Card 008 (Sophie Gamand)*

---

### Principle 008: Eye-Level Perspective
**The camera should be at the subject's level, not above.**

Shooting from above creates distance and hierarchy. Shooting at eye level creates equality and empathy. The viewer should feel like they're with the animal, not observing from a position of superiority.

*Source: Card 006, Card 008*

---

## Atmosphere Principles

### Principle 009: Warm Light Creates Trust
**Warm color temperatures (golden, amber, soft white) create emotional connection.**

Cold light feels clinical. Warm light feels human. The Hero should use warm lighting to create approachability and trust. This doesn't mean everything must be golden — but the overall tone should lean warm.

*Source: Card 009 (Blade Runner 2049), Card 010 (Walter Mitty)*

---

### Principle 010: Atmosphere Creates Depth
**Haze, glow, and soft gradients create emotional depth more effectively than sharp focus.**

Atmospheric elements — subtle glow, gentle haze, warm gradients — create depth and emotion without requiring sharp, high-contrast imagery. The Hero should feel warm and inviting, not clinical.

*Source: Card 009 (Roger Deakins)*

---

### Principle 011: Light Reveals, Not Decorates
**Light should reveal the subject's emotion and texture, not create visual effects.**

Every lighting choice should answer: "What does this reveal about the subject?" If the lighting doesn't serve the story, it's decoration. Remove it.

*Source: Card 009, Card 010*

---

## Typography Principles

### Principle 012: Typography Confirms, Doesn't Lead
**When photography is strong, text should support, not compete.**

The photograph communicates the emotion. The headline confirms it. The text ratio should be approximately 80/20 (image/text) for emotional sections. Words should be clean, confident, and minimal.

*Source: Card 011 (Kinfolk), Card 012 (TIME)*

---

### Principle 013: Simplicity Equals Recognition
**Complex designs are forgettable. Simple, confident designs are memorable.**

The Hero should be immediately recognizable. This comes from consistent visual language, not complexity. The composition should be simple, direct, and confident — the viewer should understand PawGuard within seconds.

*Source: Card 012 (TIME Magazine)*

---

## Motion Principles

### Principle 014: Imperceptible Motion
**The most effective web motion is motion the viewer doesn't consciously notice.**

Slow, subtle movement creates emotional depth without distraction. Motion should enhance, not announce. If the viewer notices the animation, it's too much.

*Source: Card 013 (Slow Dolly), Card 015 (Terrence Malick)*

---

### Principle 015: Organic Rhythm
**Perfect stillness feels artificial. Slight, organic micro-movement creates life.**

The interface should feel alive and human, not robotic. A slight float, a gentle drift, a breathing rhythm — barely perceptible, but felt. The rhythm should be natural, like breathing.

*Source: Card 015 (Breathing Movement)*
