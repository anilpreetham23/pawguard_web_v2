# PawGuard Hero Concepts

> Three distinct creative directions for the Hero section.
> Each concept is a complete emotional world, not just a layout variation.
> All concepts follow the Design Principles derived from Research Cards 001–015.

---

## Concept A: "The Quiet Morning"

### Emotional Core
**HOPE** — The feeling of a new day, a fresh start, a second chance.

### Visual Story (First 10 Seconds)
```
User opens website
    ↓
Warm morning light fills the frame
    ↓
A golden retriever sits in a sunlit doorway, looking outward
    ↓
A hand rests gently on the dog's back — we see the arm, not the face
    ↓
Headline appears: "Every animal deserves a second chance."
    ↓
The viewer feels: warmth, hope, possibility
    ↓
CTA: "Meet Your Companion"
```

### Photography Direction
- **Subject:** A rescued dog in a foster home doorway, golden hour light streaming in
- **Lighting:** Natural window light from the left, warm color temperature (3200K-4000K)
- **Perspective:** Eye-level with the dog, slightly low angle
- **Human presence:** A hand on the dog's back — not a face, not a pose
- **Environment:** Real home, real doorway, real morning light
- **Mood:** Quiet, hopeful, intimate, authentic

### Composition
- **Layout:** Two-column split — photograph right (55%), content left (45%)
- **Focal point:** The dog's eyes, illuminated by window light
- **Negative space:** Generous breathing room around all elements
- **Hierarchy:** Photograph → Headline → Supporting text → CTAs
- **Balance:** Asymmetric — visual weight balanced through light and color

### Color Palette
- **Primary:** Warm off-white (#FAF8F1) — the background, like morning light
- **Accent:** Soft gold (#D4A574) — the light, the warmth, the hope
- **Text:** Deep charcoal (#1A1B1E) — confident, readable, grounded
- **CTA:** Navy (#00236F) — trust, stability, action

### Typography
- **Headline:** Playfair Display, 64px, weight 900 — editorial warmth
- **Supporting:** Inter, 18px, weight 400 — clean, readable
- **CTA:** Inter, 14px, weight 600 — clear, direct

### Illustration Language
- Soft, hand-drawn paw prints at 3-5% opacity
- Subtle dog silhouette sketches in the background
- Warm, organic shapes — no hard edges

### Motion Character
- **Speed:** Slow, contemplative
- **Feeling:** Like morning light moving across a room
- **Scroll:** Gentle parallax — the photograph drifts slightly slower than the text
- **Hover:** Soft glow on CTAs, like light catching glass

### What PawGuard Is Saying
"A frightened animal becomes safe because someone chose to act. This is that moment of safety."

### Strengths
- Immediately warm and inviting
- Documentary authenticity
- Emotionally resonant without being sentimental
- Works beautifully at any screen size
- The single focal point (dog in light) is unmistakable

### Weaknesses
- May feel too calm for emergency context
- Doesn't communicate the operational/scale aspect
- The "hope" emotion may not create urgency for emergency CTA

## Concept B: "The Rescue in Progress"

### Emotional Core
**URGENCY + TRUST** — Help is coming. Someone competent is handling this.

### Visual Story (First 10 Seconds)
```
User opens website
    ↓
Dark, atmospheric background — night, rain, city lights
    ↓
A rescue vehicle's headlights cut through the darkness
    ↓
A team member kneels beside a frightened dog, gloved hand extended
    ↓
Headline appears: "When animals need help, we respond."
    ↓
The viewer feels: urgency, competence, trust
    ↓
CTA: "Report an Emergency"
```

### Photography Direction
- **Subject:** A rescue team member approaching a frightened animal at night
- **Lighting:** Available light only — vehicle headlights, street lamps, flashlight beams
- **Perspective:** Eye-level, slightly behind the rescuer (we see their back, the animal's face)
- **Human presence:** Partial — gloved hands, jacket, boots. No faces looking at camera
- **Environment:** Real street, real night, real rain (if applicable)
- **Mood:** Urgent, competent, professional, trustworthy

### Composition
- **Layout:** Full-bleed photograph with overlaid content
- **Focal point:** The animal's face, illuminated by the rescuer's light
- **Negative space:** The dark areas of the photograph — they create focus
- **Hierarchy:** Photograph → Headline (white on dark) → CTAs (crimson)
- **Balance:** The light source creates natural balance — bright subject, dark surround

### Color Palette
- **Primary:** Deep navy (#0B0C10) — the night, the seriousness
- **Accent:** Crimson (#C41A1A) — urgency, action, emergency
- **Text:** Pure white (#FFFFFF) — clarity against dark
- **Light:** Warm amber (#FFD696) — the rescuer's light, hope in darkness

### Typography
- **Headline:** Playfair Display, 56px, weight 900 — authoritative, urgent
- **Supporting:** Inter, 18px, weight 400 — clear, direct
- **CTA:** Inter, 14px, weight 700 — urgent, impossible to miss

### Illustration Language
- Sharp, precise lines — rescue routes, GPS coordinates
- Emergency iconography — sirens, location pins, shields
- High contrast, no soft edges — everything is intentional

### Motion Character
- **Speed:** Fast when needed, still when waiting
- **Feeling:** Like a heartbeat — pulse, pause, pulse
- **Scroll:** The photograph stays fixed while content scrolls over it (parallax)
- **Hover:** Crimson glow on emergency CTA — pulsing, alive

### What PawGuard Is Saying
"Right now, somewhere, a team is responding. This is what that looks like."

### Strengths
- Immediately communicates emergency capability
- High visual impact — dark backgrounds stop scrolling
- The crimson CTA is impossible to miss
- Communicates scale and operational competence
- Works powerfully for the emergency use case

### Weaknesses
- May feel too intense for non-emergency visitors
- Dark backgrounds can feel cold without warm accents
- Doesn't communicate the adoption/community side
- May alienate visitors looking for hope, not urgency

## Concept C: "The Belonging"

### Emotional Core
**COMMUNITY + CONNECTION** — You're not just adopting a pet. You're joining a family.

### Visual Story (First 10 Seconds)
```
User opens website
    ↓
Warm, golden light — afternoon, a backyard, a neighborhood
    ↓
A child sits on grass, a dog rests its head on the child's lap
    ↓
Volunteers move in the background — carrying supplies, smiling
    ↓
Headline appears: "Join the community that saves lives."
    ↓
The viewer feels: belonging, warmth, purpose
    ↓
CTA: "Get Involved"
```

### Photography Direction
- **Subject:** A child and dog in a backyard, volunteers in the background
- **Lighting:** Golden hour, backlight creating rim light on subjects
- **Perspective:** Eye-level with the child, slightly low angle
- **Human presence:** Multiple people — child, volunteers, community members
- **Environment:** Real backyard, real neighborhood, real community event
- **Mood:** Warm, inclusive, purposeful, joyful

### Composition
- **Layout:** Full-bleed photograph with content overlay
- **Focal point:** The child and dog — the emotional heart
- **Background:** Volunteers and community — the context
- **Hierarchy:** Photograph → Headline → Supporting text → CTAs
- **Balance:** The subjects are center-left, the community fills the right

### Color Palette
- **Primary:** Warm cream (#FAF6EF) — the afternoon light
- **Accent:** Forest green (#2D5016) — nature, growth, community
- **Text:** Deep brown (#2A1810) — warm, grounded, human
- **CTA:** Navy (#00236F) — trust, action

### Typography
- **Headline:** Playfair Display, 52px, weight 900 — editorial, warm
- **Supporting:** Inter, 18px, weight 400 — approachable, clear
- **CTA:** Inter, 14px, weight 600 — inviting, direct

### Illustration Language
- Soft, organic shapes — hearts, community symbols
- Hand-drawn elements — paw prints, volunteer badges
- Warm, inviting colors — no sharp edges

### Motion Character
- **Speed:** Gentle, breathing
- **Feeling:** Like a community gathering — warm, alive, present
- **Scroll:** Slow reveal — each section appears as the user scrolls
- **Hover:** Soft scale on cards, warm glow on CTAs

### What PawGuard Is Saying
"This isn't just an organization. It's a community. And you're already part of it."

### Strengths
- Immediately communicates community and belonging
- Warm and inviting — appeals to volunteers and adopters
- Shows scale without feeling corporate
- The child-dog connection is universally emotional
- Works well for adoption and volunteer flows

### Weaknesses
- May feel too casual for emergency context
- Doesn't communicate operational competence
- The "community" emotion may not create urgency
- Multiple subjects can dilute the focal point
