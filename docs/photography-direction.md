# Photography Direction & Art Buying Brief

## Project: PawGuard — Animal Rescue & Adoption Network
## Document: Creative Brief — Visual Identity Through Photography
## Status: Final

---

## 1. Core Philosophy

**Photography is not decoration. It is the primary trust signal.**

Every image must answer one question: *"Did this really happen?"*

Stock photography communicates *"we wish this was us."* Candid, documentary photography communicates *"this is us, right now."* PawGuard chooses the latter.

---

## 2. Visual Identity Through Photography

| Dimension | Direction |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Lighting** | Available / natural light. Low-light preferred. Flash only for emergency night scenes. |
| **Camera** | Handheld. Slight motion blur is acceptable — it signals *presence*, not staging. |
| **Grain** | Acceptable. Digital noise is honest. Clean = staged. |
| **Color** | Desaturated with warm tones. No teal/orange Hollywood grade. |
| **Framing** | Eye-level or slightly low (animal perspective). No wide establishing shots. |
| **Subjects** | Animals are the heroes. Humans are hands, backs, profiles — never looking at camera. |
| **Context** | Real environments: streets, backyards, vet clinics, transport vehicles. Not studios. |
| **Emotion** | Restrained. Let the situation speak. No forced smiles or sad puppy poses. |

---

## 3. Image Types Required

### 3.1 Emergency / Rescue (highest priority)

| Detail | Specification |
|--------|---------------|
| **What** | Animals being lifted, carried, transported. Gloved hands. Crates. Vehicles. |
| **Feeling** | Urgency without panic. Competence under pressure. |
| **Example subjects** | A cat in a carrier being loaded into a van. A dog on a stretcher. A team coordinating at night. |
| **What to avoid** | Injured animal close-ups. Blood. Distressed owner faces. |
| **Quantity needed** | 8-12 images |
| **Licensing note** | Model releases required for any visible human faces (but brief prefers non-face angles) |

### 3.2 Veterinary Care

| Detail | Specification |
|--------|---------------|
| **What** | Exams, treatments, recovery. Gloved hands. Monitoring equipment. |
| **Feeling** | Clinical warmth. Professional but not cold. |
| **Example subjects** | A vet listening to a dog's heart. A cat recovering in a clean kennel. A wound being dressed. |
| **What to avoid** | Surgical close-ups. Graphic wounds. Sad animals. |
| **Quantity needed** | 6-10 images |

### 3.3 Foster / Shelter Life

| Detail | Specification |
|--------|---------------|
| **What** | Animals in foster homes, shelter kennels, play areas. |
| **Feeling** | Calm. Safe. Temporary but comfortable. |
| **Example subjects** | A dog sleeping on a foster couch. A cat looking out a window. A volunteer cleaning kennels. |
| **What to avoid** | Crowded shelter shots. Chain-link fence foregrounds. Sad caged animals. |
| **Quantity needed** | 10-15 images |

### 3.4 Adoption / Happy Endings

| Detail | Specification |
|--------|---------------|
| **What** | Animals in their forever homes. With new families (from behind or side). |
| **Feeling** | Quiet joy. Not exuberance. Belonging. |
| **Example subjects** | A dog walking on a trail with a human. A cat sitting on a lap (hand visible). A family playing fetch (backs to camera). |
| **What to avoid** | Studio portraits. Tilt-shift miniature effect. Heart-shaped vignettes. |
| **Quantity needed** | 6-8 images |

### 3.5 Volunteer / Community

| Detail | Specification |
|--------|---------------|
| **What** | Volunteers in action: transporting, cleaning, walking, fundraising events. |
| **Feeling** | Purposeful. Inclusive. Ordinary people doing extraordinary things. |
| **Example subjects** | A volunteer filling water bowls. Two people lifting a crate. A team briefing before a transport run. |
| **What to avoid** | Group shots smiling at camera. Award ceremonies. |
| **Quantity needed** | 8-12 images |

### 3.6 Hero / Heroine (the homepage dispatch visual)

| Detail | Specification |
|--------|---------------|
| **What** | A single, powerful action image. A team in motion. Movement blur acceptable. |
| **Feeling** | "Help is coming." Action. Direction. Purpose. |
| **Example subjects** | A rescue vehicle at golden hour. A team walking toward a scene (backs to camera). Hands reaching into a storm drain. |
| **What to avoid** | Static poses. Logo clothing. Posed hero shots. |
| **Quantity needed** | 1-2 signature images |

## 4. Technical Specifications

| Spec | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| **Resolution** | Minimum 3000px on longest side |
| **Format** | JPEG (high quality) + DNG/RAW archive |
| **Aspect ratio** | 3:2 (horizontal), 2:3 (vertical), 16:9 (hero) |
| **Color space** | sRGB for web delivery |
| **Grain** | No artificial grain added — natural sensor noise only |
| **Retouching** | Global exposure, white balance, contrast only. No compositing, no generative fill, no object removal |
| **Metadata** | Full EXIF + caption + location + subject name (if known) + date |

---

## 5. What We Do NOT Use

- **Soldier/generic pet stock** — no "sad puppy in shelter" stock photography
- **AI-generated images** — zero tolerance. Every image must be a real moment.
- **Beauty/glamour pet photography** — no studio lighting, no bokeh portraits
- **Overlaid text on images** — text lives outside the photo, never on it
- **Clients looking at camera** — breaks the documentary fourth wall

---

## 6. Sourcing Strategy

### Primary: Commission

Work with a single documentary photographer who has:
- Experience in humanitarian or animal welfare documentation
- Comfort with low-light, handheld shooting
- Availability for 3-4 shoot days across different contexts

**Recommended approach:** 2-day block following a real rescue team + 1-day shelter/foster block + 1-day adoption event block.

### Secondary: Partner Contribution

- Ask partner shelters and rescue organizations to submit photos
- Provide a short photography guide (see section 8)
- Accept mobile phone photography if it meets the aesthetic (grain is fine)

### Tertiary: Curated Licensing

If gaps remain, license from **documentary photographers** (not stock agencies). Look for:
- Photojournalism portfolios with animal/animal welfare subjects
- Individual photographers on platforms like Magnum, NOOR, or independent photojournalists

## 8. Quick-Start Guide for Partner Submissions

Share this with partner organizations:

> **How to photograph for PawGuard**
>
> 1. Use your phone. Hold it steady.
> 2. Include hands. Show someone doing something.
> 3. Don't ask people to smile. Don't ask animals to pose.
> 4. Capture the moment before the moment — the loading, the waiting, the reaching.
> 5. Avoid cages and chain-link fences.
> 6. Natural light only.
> 7. Caption every photo: what, where, when, who.
> 8. Grain is fine. Blur is fine. Real is better than perfect.

## 9. Style Reference Matrix

| Avoid | Pursue |
|-------|--------|
| Studio lighting | Available light |
| Sharp, noise-free | Natural grain |
| Posed subjects | Candid moments |
| Wide-angle landscapes | Eye-level intimacy |
| Teal/orange grade | Warm, desaturated |
| Happy looking at camera | Purposeful looking at task |
| Dog in perfect light outdoors | Dog in real conditions |
| Clean minimalist backgrounds | Context-rich environments |
